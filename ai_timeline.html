The Evolution of AI

A journey through the milestones, winters, and breakthroughs that shaped Artificial Intelligence. Click any event to explore its historical context.

1950
The Turing Test ▼

British mathematician Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," introducing the "Imitation Game" (now known as the Turing Test). This milestone established the foundational question of AI: Can machines think?

1956
The Dartmouth Workshop ▼

Organized by John McCarthy and Marvin Minsky, this summer research project officially coined the term "Artificial Intelligence." It is widely considered the founding event of the AI field as a distinct academic and technological discipline.

1966
ELIZA ▼

Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT creates ELIZA, one of the first natural language processing programs. By using simple pattern matching, it simulated a psychotherapist, proving that humans could form deep emotional attachments to machines.

1974-1980
The First AI Winter ▼

Cause: Early promises failed to materialize due to a severe lack of processing power and data.


Impact: Government funding (especially from DARPA) collapsed. AI research stalled dramatically, forcing researchers to shift their focus to smaller, more narrowly defined sub-fields to survive.

1980s
Expert Systems Boom ▼

AI found widespread commercial success through "Expert Systems"—software programs designed to mimic the decision-making ability of human experts in specific, rules-heavy fields like medicine, chemistry, and finance, temporarily ending the first AI winter.

1987-1993
The Second AI Winter ▼

Cause: Dedicated, expensive AI hardware (like LISP machines) became abruptly obsolete as standard desktop computers from IBM and Apple became cheaper and more powerful.


Impact: Corporate funding collapsed once again. The term "AI" became somewhat taboo in the tech industry, often replaced by terms like "machine learning" to secure funding.

1997
IBM Deep Blue ▼

IBM's specialized supercomputer, Deep Blue, defeats reigning world chess champion Garry Kasparov. This was a massive public relations milestone, demonstrating that immense computational brute force could outmatch human intuition in highly complex, rules-based games.

2012
The AlexNet Breakthrough ▼

Researchers Geoffrey Hinton, Ilya Sutskever, and Alex Krizhevsky create AlexNet, a deep neural network that demolished the competition in the ImageNet computer vision contest.


Key catalyst: Nvidia GPUs (Graphics Processing Units) proved uniquely capable of handling the massive parallel calculations needed to train neural networks rapidly, kicking off the modern deep learning revolution.

2015
Cloud Computing Era ▼

Tech infrastructure companies like Amazon (AWS) and Google (GCP) began offering vast, scalable computing power over the cloud. Researchers no longer needed multi-million-dollar on-premise supercomputers; they could rent server space to train complex models, accelerating global AI research exponentially.

2017
Transformer Architecture ▼

Google researchers published the seminal "Attention Is All You Need" paper, introducing the Transformer architecture. By allowing models to weigh the importance of all words in a sentence simultaneously, this framework paved the exact path for modern Large Language Models (LLMs).

2022-Present
Generative AI Boom ▼

OpenAI releases ChatGPT to the public, sparking a massive, global arms race in generative AI among tech giants like Google, Microsoft, and Meta. AI transitions from a backend research tool to a mainstream, daily consumer application, revolutionizing industries worldwide.
